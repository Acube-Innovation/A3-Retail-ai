{#- The sticker that goes on the device itself. Read by a scanner, not by a person: the barcode carries the job card number, which is what the technician's screen and the delivery counter both search by. The two lines of text are for the human holding the tray — whose phone is this, and which phone is it. Sized for a 40 x 20mm label, because it lives on the back of a customer's phone and a bigger one covers a camera. Everything on it earns its place: a label that needs a second glance to tell two Samsungs apart is not worth printing. The barcode carries `26-00053` rather than the whole `JC-SVP-26-00053`. See `a3_scan_code` — the `JC` and the branch are the same on every sticker a branch prints, so they cost bars and say nothing, and the search finds the card either way. At this size that is the difference between bars a scanner reads first time and bars it has to be coaxed over. THE ROLL IS TWO ACROSS, and that is why this page is as wide as the whole liner rather than one sticker. Sent a 40mm page, the printer centres it on the web and it comes out straddling the gap between the two stickers — half the label on each, which is what the shop first saw. Nothing on the printer fixes that: it decides where a narrow page goes. So the page is the full liner and each sticker is placed on it by hand, which puts the position under this template's control and makes it something that can be measured off a test print. Both stickers are printed, with the same job card on each. A feed advances the whole row whether one is used or two, so the second is spent either way — one goes on the phone, the other on the job bag it travels in. -#} {#- ---------------------------------------------------------------------- The roll. Measure these with a ruler if the stock ever changes, and nothing else in this file needs touching. `web_in` is in inches because the page box is; see the note on `@page` below. ------------------------------------------------------------------- -#} {%- set web_in = 3.39 -%} {#- 86.1mm: the liner, to the nearest hundredth of an inch.
